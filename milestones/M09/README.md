## The result this milestone should be remembered for

**`10/25`.** The predicted number. M08b's exact number. Published over a
**fired falsifier**.

The goldens control run came back at ten of twenty-five — inside the
pre-registered band `[8, 12]`, on the predicted value, identical to what M08b
published. A reader of that cell alone reports *no change*. Underneath it two
cases moved in opposite directions: **`grounded-017` lost a majority**
(`[P P P]` → `[P F F]`) and **`entitlement-011` gained one** (`[P F F]` →
`[P P F]`). The first of those is **F4.2**, a pre-registered falsifier of the
claim, and it fired.

SPEC/09 moved the two direction predicates **into** F4 and demoted the count to
a side-prediction in all three of its sites for exactly this reason, in these
words:

> `N` is a sum over 25 cases and is insensitive to compensating movement: three
> cases newly failing and three newly passing leaves `N` exactly where it was,
> inside the band, with six cases having moved on a prompt change.

It happened at two cases, on the first run after the change. **The instrument
that caught it is the one that was promoted, and the instrument that missed it
is the one that was demoted** — a week earlier, on an argument, with no run in
view. That is the most transferable thing in this milestone and it is worth more
than either falsifier.

## The verdict

**The claim is FAILED, on two independent falsifiers.**

> **A rule delta disposed by its owning seat into an executable control makes the
> deployed service fail a gate for a reason a reader can trace from the rule to
> the failing assert; and the fix, made where the rule points, makes it pass,
> with nothing else moving.**

| | falsifier | reading | where |
|---|---|---|---|
| **F1** | a positive disclosure case passes **before** the fix | **FIRED** — `disclosure-103`, 2 of 3 | PR 4 · ADR-075 amendment 5 |
| **F2** | a positive case fails after the fix | clean | PR 4 |
| **F3** | the gate does not block then permit | clean — exit 1, then exit 0 | PR 4 |
| **F4** | the fix moved something else | **FIRED** — `grounded-017`, clause 2 | PR 4b · ADR-075 amendment 6 |
| **F5** | the negative case discloses after the fix | clean | PR 4 |

**F1** fired on `disclosure-103`, which asks for *"a paragraph I can publish"*
about the Cedar Point Rowing Finals. The **pre-fix** service volunteered a
disclosure on two of three samples, unprompted, in two different wordings
(*"This summary was generated by an AI assistant based on available event
data."* and *"This summary was AI-authored."*). Both carry `AI` as a whole word,
the pack's phrasing list matches, and the case passed run A. The control was
already satisfied there — `rules/MER-AI-0001.yaml`'s own recorded hazard, which
is why F1 is the falsifier ADR-075 led with.

**F4** fired on `grounded-017`, on `tokens_out` at a tier nothing in this
milestone touched.

**The count landing on its predicted value is a side-prediction hitting, not a
partial success**, and this journal does not read it as mitigation anywhere. The
claim is not re-scoped, narrowed or restated to fit what survived. *What must not
happen* forbids a claim rewritten to match its outcome **in either direction**.

**What did hold, stated plainly and not as consolation.** The rule *was* disposed
end-to-end. `pave rules trace MER-AI-0001` walks law → rule → scope → control →
seven cases → the asserts with no step supplied by hand, at `chain: RESOLVED`,
exit 0 — where before PR 4 the same command stopped at `no-control` and reported
`chain: NOT RESOLVED`. The gate blocked at exit 1 on run A's verdict and
permitted at exit 0 on run B's, through the deployed gateway, at the guardrail
versions M08b read, with no deploy taken. Every positive case passes after the
fix and the negative case discloses nothing. **The mechanism works; the claim
about it is false as written**, and those are different sentences.

## Three findings

Each is written once, here, as a finding. None is a debt row, and none is
repeated in the table below.

### 1. Majority reading masks instability — and it did so three times, in two PRs

This milestone read three side-predictions by majority and all three hid
something the underlying samples said plainly.

| where | the majority reading | what the samples said |
|---|---|---|
| `disclosure-101`, run A | **FAIL** — 1 of 3 passed, so F1 does not fire on it | the pre-fix service volunteered a disclosure **at least once**. With `disclosure-103`, **two of the five positive cases disclosed before the fix** |
| the goldens count | **10/25** — in band, on the predicted value, *"no change"* | two cases moved in opposite directions; one of them is a fired falsifier |
| refusals | **1 of 25** by majority — in band, and **down** from M08b's 2 | **4 of 25** refused at least once, against M08b's 2. Three cases now separate the two estimators where none did before |

**This is a defect in how results are READ, not in the runs.** Every run is
committed as-run, every number above is read out of it by committed code, and no
sample is in dispute. What differs is the aggregation, and in all three cases the
majority is the lossier one.

The repository already knew: ADR-035 picked **refused-at-least-once** as its
estimator for exactly this reason, and M08b's own journal concluded that *"a k=3
majority on a one-sample margin is not a stable instrument for a two-case
prediction."* SPEC/09 acted on it for the direction predicates and not for the
side-predictions beside them.

**Carried forward as a pre-registration constraint, for M09b to write into its
spec:** *a side-prediction read by majority carries an at-least-once companion
reading, published beside it.* Not a debt with a trigger — a rule about how the
next milestone's predictions are written down, owed before its first run.

### 2. The joining readers are the least-checked layer

Between a run and a published record sits a *joining reader* — a committed script
that takes measurements from one place, estimates or baselines from another, and
writes a record a human then quotes. This milestone found defects in two of them,
and each would have published a false reading.

**`milestones/M08b/residual_attribution.py` has no era pin.** Its sibling
`milestones/M08/residual_differential.py` has one (`identical_to_head`), and the
sibling's guard **fired correctly** in the diff that moved the prompt. The
M08b reader re-priced in silence: its published attribution went from
*"provider-side framing, 501 of 463 signed (92.9%)"* to **552 of 402 (78.6%)**
with **no run taken between the two readings**. Two sibling records, one input,
one hazard, one guarded. `92.9%` is not corrected — it is the reading of that run
and it stands, era-pinned to M08b's prompt (PR 4b annotated it in place).
**`78.6%` is the artifact**, of a prompt that run never sent.

**`milestones/M08b/fresh_join.py`'s direction block compares against M08, not
M08b.** Its `count.falsifiers` keys are literally named
`3_of_3_pass_at_m08_failing_by_majority` and
`0_of_3_fail_at_m08_passing_by_majority`, and its comparison base is a **module
constant** pointing at `milestones/M08/rescore-join.json`. Run over M09's answers
it wrote, and `milestones/M09/fresh-join.json` now publishes:

```json
"falsifiers": {
  "N_outside_band": false,
  "3_of_3_pass_at_m08_failing_by_majority": [],
  "0_of_3_fail_at_m08_passing_by_majority": []
}
```

Both lists **empty**, on the run where **F4.2 fired**. That is true of M08 —
against M08, `grounded-017` did not pass 3-of-3 — and false of M08b, which is the
baseline F4's clauses name. **Reading F4 out of that record would have published
CLEAN OVER A FIRED FALSIFIER.** `milestones/M09/f4.py` exists because PR 4b did
not take it at face value, and reads the baseline from
`evals/history/m08b-tools-goldens.json` — the entry `README_GOLDENS` pins the
`m08b` row to.

**And a third stale constant in the same block, found while verifying the
second.** That record also publishes `"band": [7, 14]` and `"predicted": 12` —
**M08b's** side-prediction, not M09's pre-registered `[8, 12]` and `10`. Three
constants of another milestone's, inside a record filed under `milestones/M09/`.

**What no instrument holds, stated precisely — and the obvious version of it is
wrong.** The reading this close reached for first was *no seat holds a key over
that layer*. **Measured, that is false**, and the correction matters more than
the claim did: `python -m pave.cli gate two-key --changed` puts `fresh_join.py`,
`residual_attribution.py`, `f4.py`, `milestones/M09/fresh-join.json` and
`milestones/M08b/residual-attribution.json` all on *the M08 census* rule at
**three keys** (AI Quality, Platform Engineering, Security). Three seats signed
the bytes of every one of those records. Publishing the unmeasured version would
have handed M09b a rule to write that already exists.

What the keys cannot see is the failure mode that actually happened: **the rule
fires on an edit, and neither defect arrived through one.** `fresh_join.py` ran
**unchanged** and carried another milestone's constants into a new record;
`residual_attribution.py` was **never opened** while a prompt constant moved
underneath it. A key certifies that a record's bytes changed and who agreed to
it. Nothing asks whether the baseline *inside* the record is the one this
milestone pre-registered. That is the gap, and it is narrower and more fixable
than "no seat holds a key".

### 3. The claim was confounded before the first call

F1 did not discover that the fix failed. It discovered that **the premise the
claim rested on was not true when the claim was written.**

Two of the five positive disclosure cases volunteered a disclosure at least once
**before** the fix. The pack's own README recorded the measured basis for
expecting none — *"of the 56 committed M08b samples that carry the key, **none**
is non-null"* — and that was true. What it could not see is that **no M08b golden
case asks for editorial copy.** The disclosure pack's whole subject is a request
shape the goldens do not contain, so 56 null samples were 56 samples of a
different question. The premise was confirmed on the only evidence available, and
the evidence was off-subject.

**The honest result, recorded here for M09b to pre-register and not retrofitted
onto M09:**

> The fix moved disclosure on editorial-copy requests from **intermittent** to
> **reliable**: 7 of 7 after, against 4 FAIL / 3 PASS before, with two of five
> positive cases already disclosing sometimes.

That is a real result about a real control, and it is a **weaker claim** than the
one registered, which said the delta was total. It is written down as the weaker
claim rather than substituted for the stronger one, because a claim replaced
after its falsifier fires is a claim chosen to match its outcome. **M09 closes on
the claim it registered, which failed.**

## What broke?

**The demo artifact did not run, for three PRs.** `SPEC/09`'s own demo block
shipped

```
python -m pave.cli gate decide milestones/M09/verdict-pre-fix.json
```

The gate takes verdicts on `--verdicts`; a bare positional is parsed and then
ignored, so the command reports *"no verdict files supplied — the gate does not
pass on an empty argument list"* and exits **2**, the harness-failure code, where
the spec published exit 1 and exit 0. PR 4 ran the correct form and committed its
transcripts, so nothing was measured wrongly — **the defect is in the one block a
stranger is handed**, and nothing executed it. Fixed here, and
`tests/test_documented_commands.py` now runs every command in every spec's *Demo
artifact* section and compares its exit code to the one published beside it. The
scope is derived from the specs rather than listed, so M09b's demo block joins
the check by existing.

**The `rules trace` output published in that same section was two PRs stale.** It
printed `status proposed` / `chain: NOT RESOLVED` under the heading *"Today that
is"* — true at PR 2 and false since PR 4 disposed the rule. It is now dated to
the window it describes and the post-disposition reading is printed beside it.
The **predicted** output block above it is deliberately left as written: it is
labelled a prediction, it is a pre-registration artifact, and filling its letters
in now would be editing a prediction to match the outcome.

**PR 7's branch name does not match its content, and that was deliberate.** The
seventh PR landed on `m09-pr2-followups` — a branch named for PR 2. It was
**kept rather than renamed** because the branch carries the provenance the breach
record rests on: ADR-075 amendment 4's whole argument is that the four
corrections were dispositioned *before PR 2 merged* and missed that diff in a
**merge race**, and the branch is where that sequence is legible. A branch renamed
to `m09-pr7-*` would read as a seventh PR planned from the start, which is
precisely the reading the breach record exists to refuse. The mismatch is the
record; renaming it would have been tidying away the evidence.

**Nine statements this milestone made about its own state were false when made,
and its measurements were wrong none.** ADR-075 amendment 3 §2 found four
claims this repository made about its own work that a check disproved; §11 found
a fifth, written by the fix for the fourth; amendment 4 §4 found a sixth — a
follow-up instruction premised on PR 2 being open when it had already merged.
**This close adds the seventh, eighth and ninth**: the demo block's published
exit codes, the `rules trace` output that had been stale since PR 4, and this
journal's own first draft claiming no ADR decision text was rewritten when PR 7
had rewritten decision 5 §4's blockquote (see *Decisions*). Every one of the nine was found by **running or reading a check**,
never by re-reading the sentence that made the claim. That pattern is worth more
than any individual correction on the list, and it is the argument for the one
new check this PR adds — the two it found had each survived every reader this
milestone put in front of them.

**The deletability audit outranked both seat rounds, and that is new.** Two
rounds, seven seats, twelve reports, 86 findings — and the audit found that
**18 of the first 40 checks the PR added were silent when deleted**, nearly every
one of them a remedy written in answer to a seat's own plant. A seat round proves
a defect exists. It does not prove a fix works, and nothing in this repository's
process was asking it to. The standing rule that came out of it — *a remedy ships
with the mutation that removes it and the test that names the failure, in the
same diff* — is why PR 4b's audit came back **17 of 17 RED** and this PR's came
back clean.

**And this PR's own audit ran a narrower suite than the gate does — found on
`make check`, after the audit reported 12 of 12 red.** The audit's `_run_suite`
runs `pytest`; `make check` is `pave check`, which runs **ruff first**. Two
`E741` ambiguous-variable warnings — one in the new check, one in the audit
script itself — were invisible to twelve mutations and a full green `pytest
tests/`, and stopped the gate dead. Nothing about the mutations was wrong; the
**tree they were measured on is one the gate refuses**. That is ADR-075
amendment 3 §11's lesson one notch in: §11 found the *machine* answering for a
guard, and this is the *suite the audit runs* answering for it. Fixed, the audit
re-run whole against the linted tree, and recorded as a debt — an audit whose
suite is narrower than the gate's can report a mutation red on a tree CI would
never accept.

**The first CI run found two defects no single-machine instrument could.** Both
were masked by the machine the guards were measured on: `boto3` imported at
module scope made every pre-flight refusal unreachable on a runner without the
SDK, and an estimator read `platform/infra/cdk.out/` — a gitignored build
directory that resolves here and in no clone. The deletability audit reported the
second **SILENT**, correctly, because both paths resolve on this machine. **This
PR's first CI run should have preceded its last seat round, not followed its
merge command.**
